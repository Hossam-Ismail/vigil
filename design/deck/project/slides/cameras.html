Cameras

Plug in the cameras you already have

01 · IP CAMERAS

Found for you

Vigil scans the network for ONVIF cameras and fills in the RTSP address. Reolink, Tapo, Hikvision, Dahua, Axis, Hanwha, UniFi, Eufy.

02 · PHONE OR LAPTOP

Open a link

Any phone or laptop becomes a camera from a browser tab. Nothing to install. Great for a quick test.

03 · STREAM URL

Paste the address

HLS (.m3u8), MJPEG, RTSPS or any stream FFmpeg can read.

04 · USB WEBCAM

Plug it in

Any webcam on the computer running Vigil.

05 · RECORDED VIDEO

Try it on past footage

Point Vigil at a video file and it plays it back in real time, so you can test a watch before it goes live.

06 · EVERYTHING ELSE

Restream it

Cameras without RTSP can be restreamed through go2rtc or MediaMTX, and Vigil reads that.

Video and camera passwords stay on site. Vigil runs on the machine next to your cameras.

Vigil

10

Every way to get video into Vigil today, all in the Add camera screen. 1) IP cameras: Vigil sends an ONVIF discovery probe on the local network, lists the cameras that answer, and fills in the RTSP address from built-in templates for Reolink, Tapo, Hikvision and Annke, Dahua, Amcrest and Lorex, Axis, Hanwha Wisenet, UniFi Protect (RTSPS) and Eufy; it reads the low-resolution sub-stream to keep decoding cheap. 2) Phone or laptop: open Vigil's camera page in a browser and that device streams frames to Vigil, no app. 3) Stream URL: HTTP MJPEG, HLS (.m3u8), RTSPS or anything FFmpeg can open. 4) USB webcam by device index on the machine running Vigil. 5) A video file, looped and played back in real time, which is how a watch can be tried on recorded footage. 6) Anything else, such as cameras without RTSP, can be restreamed through go2rtc or MediaMTX and added as an RTSP stream. Camera credentials and video stay on the local machine.
