Who Vigil is for: anyone who runs physical operations and already has cameras: manufacturing, logistics and warehousing, retail and restaurants, property and facilities (and homes, at the small end). The problem: the moments that matter happen on camera (a line stalls, a dock gets a truck, a queue grows, a gate is left open), but cameras only record; catching those moments live takes a person watching screens, so most footage is reviewed only after something went wrong. What it's worth, all sourced: about one billion surveillance cameras were forecast to be deployed worldwide by 2021 (IHS Markit, reported by The Verge, https://www.theverge.com/2019/12/9/21002515/surveillance-cameras-globally-us-china-amount-citizens); the median annual wage of a U.S. security guard was $38,020 in May 2025, for one person covering one shift (U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics, Occupational Outlook Handbook, https://www.bls.gov/ooh/protective-service/security-guards.htm); the global video analytics market is projected to reach $37.84 billion by 2030, growing 19.5% a year from 2025 (Grand View Research, https://www.grandviewresearch.com/press-release/global-video-analytics-market). Vigil turns cameras a business already owns into watchers for exactly what it cares about, without new hardware, a data team or someone staring at a monitor.
