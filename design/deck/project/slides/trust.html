Vigil is built to be safe to put in front of the public. What it refuses: a safety policy runs before any model is built. Requests that would identify, recognize or follow a particular person, single people out by gender, race, ethnicity, religion, age, disability, pregnancy, orientation or appearance, look at bodies or nudity, watch bathrooms, changing rooms or other people's homes, monitor people covertly, guess emotions, intentions, health or status, or single out children are refused with a plain reason and a neutral rewording (the example at the bottom is the app's real response). The check runs three times: deterministic rules on the words, the AI compiler's own policy verdict, and the rules again on the finished watch, including edits. Refusals are counted by category only, never stored as text. Your footage: private mode is on by default, so no image is ever sent to a cloud model and the local model does all the work; faces (the head of every detected person) are pixelated in alert snapshots and clips, which are what gets texted or shown; background frames are deleted after 7 days and alerts, snapshots and clips after 30 (both adjustable); camera passwords never leave the machine; the app only listens on this computer (127.0.0.1). There is no Vigil server: nothing about your cameras reaches us. If someone turns private mode off to use the Claude brain, only the few frames it labels or double-checks are sent to the Claude API, never the live stream.
